Finding us and getting in

Where is My Dealer?

243 3rd Street North in Bayport, MN 55003. That's Highway 95 right through downtown Bayport. We're next door to the American Legion and the post office, across from city hall and the police station. The sign sits flat on the brick, so look for the face with the hat.

Is there a dispensary near Stillwater?

Yes, us. We're a few minutes south of Stillwater on Highway 95, right across the river from Wisconsin. From Highway 36, take the Stillwater/Bayport exit (MN-95) and head south about 2 minutes. We'll be on your left. From I-94, take Exit 258 and go about 4 miles north on 95. We'll be on your right.

Where do I park?

The building has entrances at both the front and the back, so come in whichever way is easier. There's a paved lot behind the building: turn onto 1st Ave N, then take a left down the alley. There's also street parking out front on 3rd Street (Highway 95), and a public lot at City Hall across the street.

What are your hours?

Noon to 8, every day. Longer hours are on the way, and I'll update this page when they change.

Are you a chain?

No. It's one locally owned store. I got a Minnesota social equity retail license, put a second mortgage on the house, and built most of this place myself. Wabasha is next, but right now it's just Bayport.

Is this real cannabis or a hemp shop?

Real cannabis. We're licensed by the Minnesota Office of Cannabis Management (OCM), Cannabis Retailer License DIS-L24-000241 (verify on OCM's licensed-business list). Our flower is grown as cannabis by licensed Minnesota cultivators and tracked by the state from seed to sale. We also carry some hemp-derived products, and those are labeled as hemp so you always know which is which.

ID and age

Do I need to be 21 to shop?

Yes. Everyone has to be 21 or older with a valid ID. We check ID every visit, no exceptions.

Can I bring someone under 21 with me?

No. Nobody under 21 is allowed in the store, even with a parent or another adult.

What ID do you accept?
  • A driver's license or state ID from any U.S. state
  • A Canadian driver's license or ID
  • A Tribal ID
  • A U.S. passport or passport card
  • A Minnesota instructional permit, if you're 21 or older
  • A passport, if you're a foreign national

We can't take expired IDs, altered IDs, or a foreign driver's license or national ID card (that's state law, not us being picky). The photo has to match you.

Wisconsin, Hudson, and crossing the river

I live in Wisconsin. Can I buy at My Dealer?

Yes. Any adult 21 or older with a valid ID can buy here, including Wisconsin residents, within Minnesota's limits. Just know that what you buy is legal in Minnesota only. Cannabis isn't legal in Wisconsin, and you can't take it across the river. For the full rundown on the border rule, where you can legally use it in Minnesota, and what's legal back home, see our guide for Wisconsin shoppers.

Is there a cannabis dispensary in Hudson, WI?

Not a licensed one. Cannabis isn't legal in Wisconsin, so there are no licensed cannabis dispensaries there. A shop on that side of the river calling itself a dispensary is a hemp, smoke, or tobacco-type store, not a state-licensed cannabis retailer. A close licensed option is on the Minnesota side: us, in Bayport, about 10 minutes from downtown Hudson.

Paying

Do you take credit cards?

No. We take cash, or your debit card through our cashless ATM. We also have a regular ATM on site. The cashless ATM has a $3 fee, the same idea as a regular ATM. It runs in $10 steps, so your withdrawal rounds up to the nearest $10 and we hand you the difference back in cash.

Why don't you give coins?

We don't use coins. After tax, every total rounds up to the nearest dollar, and that little bit goes straight to the employees as their tip.

Do I need to tip?

No. The roundup is the tip. Nobody here expects anything extra.

What taxes are on cannabis in Minnesota?

Adult-use cannabis products carry Minnesota's 15% cannabis gross receipts tax, plus the 6.875% state sales tax and any local sales tax where you buy. Every tax shows on your receipt.

Limits and the law

How much cannabis can I buy at once?

Minnesota caps a single purchase at:

  • 2 ounces of flower (pre-rolls count by weight)
  • 8 grams of concentrate (vapes count here)
  • 800 mg of THC in edibles, shared across cannabis edibles, hemp edibles, and hemp THC drinks

Each edible serving can have up to 10 mg THC and each package up to 200 mg. Drinks top out at 10 mg per serving and two servings per container.

For carrying: up to 2 ounces of flower in public, up to 2 pounds of flower at home, and up to 8 grams of concentrate and 800 mg of edible THC.

Can I use it in the parking lot or my car?

No. Minnesota law doesn't allow anyone to use cannabis in a motor vehicle, parked or moving, driver or passenger (Minn. Stat. 342.09, subd. 1(b)(2)). That includes our lot. For the ride home, keep products in the trunk or rear cargo area unless they're still sealed in the original package. Use it at home or on private property where it's allowed.

Can I drive after using cannabis?

No. Driving while impaired by cannabis is illegal in Minnesota and carries DWI penalties, even if you feel fine. Cannabis slows reaction time and judgment, and edibles can last 6 to 12 hours. Get a ride or wait it out.

Where can I legally use cannabis in Minnesota?

Adults 21 and older can use it:

  • In a private home, including the yard
  • On private property that isn't open to the public, unless the owner says no
  • At a business or event licensed for on-site use

You can't use it:

  • In any motor vehicle, parked or moving
  • Smoking or vaping anywhere Minnesota's Clean Indoor Air Act bans smoking
  • Smoking or vaping inside an apartment or other multifamily building, including balconies and patios
  • At public schools, state prisons, on federal property (like national parks), or in federally subsidized housing
  • Anywhere a minor would breathe the smoke or vapor
  • In public places

Cities can add their own rules. In Bayport, smoking or vaping cannabis in public places is banned, and that includes our parking lot. Bayport also bans open cannabis drinks on streets, sidewalks, and parking areas. There's no place to use it at My Dealer.

How should I carry cannabis in the car?

Keep it out of reach. OCM says products should ride in the trunk or rear cargo area unless they're still sealed in the original, labeled package. Nobody can use cannabis in a vehicle, parked or moving, driver or passenger, and driving high is a DWI. Don't take it across state lines, even into another legal state. That's a federal crime.

Can I grow my own?

Yes, if you're 21 or older. Minnesota allows up to eight plants per residence, with no more than four of them mature, flowering plants at one time (Minn. Stat. 342.09, subd. 2). They have to be at the primary residence of someone 21+, in an enclosed, locked space the public can't see. The limit is per home, not per person. Making concentrates at home with butane or other volatile solvents isn't allowed. We carry cannabis seeds, so ask what's in stock.

Cannabis basics

What does THC % mean?

It's how much of the product's weight is THC. Most of the THC in raw flower is THCA, which turns into THC when it's heated. So labels and our menu show total THC, which Minnesota defines as THCA x 0.877, plus all forms of THC (mostly delta-9). Example: flower with 20% THCA and 0.5% delta-9 THC works out to about 18% total THC. That's the number to compare. A higher number means stronger, not better. Aroma, terpenes, and your own experience matter just as much. For edibles and drinks, look at milligrams (mg) per serving and per package instead.

What's the difference between hemp and cannabis?

Legally, it comes down to THC. Botanically they're the same plant, Cannabis sativa. Under Minnesota law, a growing hemp plant has no more than 0.3% delta-9 THC by dry weight, and above that it's cannabis (Minn. Stat. 342.01). Federal law uses the same 0.3% delta-9 line today. On December 11, 2026, it switches to 0.3% total THC, which counts THCA too.

Minnesota also allows lower-potency hemp edibles and hemp THC drinks. OCM regulates them with the same 21+ age limit, testing, and labeling rules, and they count toward the same 800 mg limit as cannabis edibles. Everything we sell is labeled as either cannabis or hemp.

What's the difference between indica, sativa, and hybrid?

Not as much as people think. Indica, sativa, and hybrid mostly describe a plant's family tree and how it grows. Studies comparing those labels with genetic and lab data found they often don't line up (Sawler et al., 2015; Watts et al., 2021; Smith et al., 2022). Strains have been cross-bred for decades, and the same name can differ from one grower to the next.

Our menu still shows which way a strain leans (Sativa, Sativa-dominant, Hybrid, Indica-dominant, Indica), because it's familiar shorthand. For a better read, look at:

  • Total THC and CBD on the label
  • The top terpenes, when listed, and how the flower smells to you
  • The format: inhaled products usually start within minutes, while edibles can take up to 2 hours
  • Your own notes on batches you liked

Ask a budtender about a specific batch.

How much of an edible should I take?

Start low and go slow. If you're new, or it's been a while, start with 2.5 to 5 mg of THC or less. That's a quarter to half of a typical 10 mg gummy. Per OCM, edibles usually take 30 minutes to 2 hours to kick in, peak around 3 to 4 hours, and last 6 to 12 hours. Don't take more just because you don't feel it yet. Wait at least 2 hours before you think about more. Too much can mean hours of anxiety, nausea, or vomiting, so patience pays off.

Is it okay to mix cannabis and alcohol?

We don't recommend it. OCM says mixing cannabis with alcohol, prescription medications, or other substances can sharply increase impairment and the risk of effects you didn't expect. Pick one.

How should I store cannabis at home?

Keep flower in an airtight container, like the original packaging or a glass jar, in a cool, dark place. Heat, light, and air wear down aroma and potency over time, so a cupboard beats a sunny windowsill. A relative humidity of around 58 to 62% keeps flower from drying out or getting too damp, and we sell small two-way humidity packs that hold it in that range. Most of all, keep it locked up in its child-resistant package. See How do I keep cannabis away from kids and pets?

Can your budtenders give medical advice?

No. We can talk about products, formats, aroma, and what's on the label, but we're not doctors, and we don't diagnose or recommend cannabis for any condition. Cannabis can change how some medications work, so if you have a health condition or take medication, talk with your health care provider or pharmacist first.

Nothing hits like it used to. What should I do?

That's common with regular use. Your body adapts to THC over time, so the same amount can start to feel like less. Before trading up to stronger products, a lot of regulars try a short break of a few days. When you come back, start with a small amount and work up slowly. If cutting back feels hard, or cannabis is getting in the way at home or work, talk with a health care provider, or call SAMHSA's free National Helpline at 1-800-662-4357.

Should I try a product with both CBD and THC?

It's worth asking about if you're new to THC or it sometimes feels like too much. CBD isn't intoxicating the way THC is. Some people who are new or sensitive to THC pick balanced products with both, and many say the experience feels gentler. It doesn't work the same for everyone, and CBD doesn't cancel THC out: a gummy with 5 mg THC and 5 mg CBD still has 5 mg of THC, so dose by the THC number. Start low either way, and ask a budtender to show you the balanced options.

What are CBG and CBN?

They're two of the minor cannabinoids, compounds the plant makes in smaller amounts than THC and CBD. CBG starts out as a building block: in its raw form, the plant turns most of it into other cannabinoids, so mature flower usually has only a little. CBN forms as THC ages and breaks down, which is one reason older flower can test higher in CBN. Neither is usually described as intoxicating the way THC is. Research on both is still early, and we don't make promises about what they do. If a product lists CBG or CBN, the label shows how many milligrams are in each serving.

How do I read a cannabis label?

Start with three things:

  • Serving vs. package. Edibles and drinks list THC per serving and for the whole package. A 100 mg package of gummies is often ten 10 mg pieces. Minnesota caps edibles at 10 mg THC per serving and 200 mg per package, and drinks at 10 mg per serving with no more than two servings per container.
  • Total THC for flower and vapes. Raw flower is mostly THCA, which turns into THC when heated. Total THC = THCA x 0.877, plus THC. Example: 20% THCA and 0.5% THC is about 18% total THC.
  • Batch and testing. Every package has a batch number and shows it passed state testing.

You'll also see the ingredients, the license number of whoever grew or made it, the universal cannabis symbol, and "Keep this product out of reach of children." If something doesn't add up, ask a budtender.

How do I keep cannabis away from kids and pets?

Keep it in its original child-resistant package, locked up and out of sight. Edibles and drinks can look like regular snacks, so store them apart from food. If a child eats cannabis, call Poison Control at 1-800-222-1222 right away, or 911 in an emergency. If it's a pet, call your vet. And don't smoke or vape anywhere a child would breathe it.

Cannabis types and terpenes

Plain-language answers about how cannabis gets classified and what terpenes are. This section covers labeling, aroma, and flavor only. It is not health advice. Sources are linked in each answer and listed at the end of this section.

What are terpenes?

Terpenes are natural aroma compounds made by many plants, including cannabis. They're a big part of why one strain smells like citrus and another like pine or pepper.

Cannabis contains hundreds of different compounds, and here's a surprise: THC and CBD on their own have almost no smell. What you're smelling is mostly terpenes. They're made in the tiny resin glands (trichomes) on the flower, and the same ones show up across nature: limonene in citrus peel, pinene in pine needles, linalool in lavender (Booth et al., 2020).

When you shop, smell the flower and check the top terpenes on the label. Plenty of people find they have favorite aromas, and that's a good way to find products you like.

Which terpenes are common in cannabis, and what do they smell like?

The most common are myrcene, beta-caryophyllene, limonene, terpinolene, pinene, linalool, and humulene. The table below lists aroma notes and where else each one occurs in nature.

How much terpene content is typical in cannabis flower?

Usually about 1% to 3% of the flower's dry weight. Published studies of cured flower range from roughly 0.5% up to about 3–4%.

  • 72 commercial medical cannabis flower samples: 0.6% to 3.3% total terpenoids by dry weight (Richins et al., 2018).
  • Flower of three chemovars: 1.28% to 2.14% (Jin et al., 2020), which notes up to about 4% reported in unfertilized flowers in earlier work.
  • Mature flowers of five cultivars: 9.3 to 13.6 mg/g, or about 0.9% to 1.4% (Booth et al., 2020).

Terpenes evaporate, so numbers depend on how the flower was grown, dried, stored, and tested. A lab label showing total terpenes is a snapshot of that batch.
