Who can buy

Any adult 21 or older with a valid, unexpired photo ID can buy at My Dealer. That includes Wisconsin residents. Out-of-state IDs work. Under Minnesota law, a valid driver's license or ID card from Minnesota, another state, or a Canadian province works, as long as it has your photo and date of birth. A U.S. passport, a valid Tribal ID, or a foreign passport also works. No ID, no sale. Minn. Stat. 342.27, subd. 4

Minnesota caps what a licensed retailer can sell to one customer in a single transaction: Minn. Stat. 342.27, subd. 2(c)

  • Up to 2 ounces of adult-use cannabis flower
  • Up to 8 grams of adult-use cannabis concentrate
  • Edible cannabis products and lower-potency hemp edibles with up to 800 milligrams of THC total

Licensed cannabis edibles in Minnesota top out at 10 mg THC per serving and 200 mg per package. Minn. Stat. 342.27, subd. 2(d) Hemp-derived edibles are capped lower: 5 mg per serving, or 10 mg per drink. Minn. Stat. 342.01, subd. 50 The same 2 oz / 8 g / 800 mg amounts are also what an adult can carry in public in Minnesota. Minn. Stat. 342.09, subd. 1(a)

The border rule: it stays in Minnesota

I'm going to be straight with you, because I'd rather lose a sale than have you get in trouble. What you buy here is legal in Minnesota only. Taking it across the St. Croix into Wisconsin is illegal.

  • Wisconsin: THC (outside of legal hemp) is a controlled substance. A first possession conviction can mean a fine of up to $1,000, up to 6 months in jail, or both. A second or later offense is a Class I felony. Wis. Stat. 961.14(4)(t), Wis. Stat. 961.41(3g)(e)
  • Federal: marijuana is still a controlled substance under federal law, and a Minnesota license doesn't change that. 21 U.S.C. 844
  • Every package we sell carries the state-required line: "This product may be unlawful outside the state of Minnesota."

So the plan is simple: buy here, use it here, and don't bring it home.

Using it legally in Minnesota

Minnesota is specific about where adults can consume. Minn. Stat. 342.09, subd. 1(a)(7) and 1(b)

  • OK: a private residence, including its yard
  • OK: private property that isn't generally open to the public, as long as the owner hasn't told you no. If you're staying with friends or renting a place, ask first. Plenty of rentals and hotels ban it, and smoking or vaping is also off-limits anywhere Minnesota's smoking ban applies.
  • OK: a business or event licensed for on-site consumption
  • Not OK: in any motor vehicle, parked or moving, driver or passenger
  • Not OK: driving under the influence
  • Not OK: public places like parks, sidewalks, or our parking lot
  • Not OK: smoking or vaping anywhere a minor would breathe it in

There is no place to consume at My Dealer. We're a retail store only.

What's legal in Wisconsin right now

Short version: Wisconsin has no adult-use cannabis and no medical cannabis program. Here's what the state's own sources say.

  • Hemp: Wisconsin law excludes hemp (as defined in Wis. Stat. 94.55) from its THC definition, so legal hemp products aren't treated as marijuana under state law. Wis. Stat. 961.14(4)(t)3. DATCP ended its hemp licensing program on January 1, 2022; growers are now licensed by USDA. DATCP still publishes labeling and method-of-sale guidance for hemp flower and CBD products. DATCP Wisconsin Hemp Resources, DATCP labeling guide (PDF)
  • CBD: possessing hemp-derived CBD doesn't require a doctor's certification in Wisconsin. Other CBD products are allowed with a physician's certification. Wis. Stat. 961.32(2m)
  • Medical: Wisconsin has no medical cannabis program. A 2025-26 medical cannabis bill, SB 534, did not pass. 2025 Senate Bill 534
  • Heads up on hemp THC: under current federal law, starting December 11, 2026, finished hemp products with more than 0.4 mg of total THC per package, and hemp products made with lab-converted cannabinoids, no longer meet the federal definition of hemp. Products with cannabinoids the cannabis plant can't make on its own lose federal hemp status earlier, on November 12, 2026. Wisconsin's own hemp definition hasn't changed. Congressional Research Service IF13136

Hemp shops in Wisconsin that call themselves dispensaries aren't state-licensed cannabis retailers. There aren't any in Wisconsin.

Hemp THC on December 11: what changes, and what doesn't

Heads up for Wisconsin shoppers. Under current federal law, starting December 11, 2026, a finished hemp product with more than 0.4 mg of total THC per package (the can, bottle, or bag it's sold in) no longer meets the federal definition of hemp. So do hemp products made with lab-converted cannabinoids. That covers most hemp THC gummies, vapes, and drinks, including delta-8. This is a federal change, not a new Wisconsin law. Wisconsin's hemp law hasn't changed, and two Wisconsin bills to regulate hemp THC products, AB 747 and SB 682, did not pass.

Minnesota has its own hemp law. The Minnesota Office of Cannabis Management says the federal change doesn't change how Minnesota defines lower-potency hemp edibles and drinks, or whether they're legal under Minnesota law. OCM also notes that many hemp products that comply with Minnesota law, such as packages with more than 0.4 mg of THC, won't meet the new federal definition, which could affect hemp businesses' banking, taxes, and interstate shipping.

Licensed Minnesota cannabis is real cannabis, not hemp: grown by licensed Minnesota cultivators and tracked by the state. It's legal under Minnesota law only, and it's still illegal under federal law and in Wisconsin. Come visit Bayport for real flower at the deli counter, along with licensed pre-rolls, vapes, and edibles. Everything you buy here, cannabis or hemp, is for use in Minnesota only.

Our read, not legal advice. Sources: 7 U.S.C. 1639o as amended by P.L. 119-37, Div. B, Sec. 781 (enacted November 12, 2025); timing under P.L. 119-103, Sec. 2019 (signed September 2, 2026); Congressional Research Service IF13136; Minnesota OCM, Federal hemp changes; Wisconsin AB 747 and SB 682. Congress could still change these dates. Bills to delay the change again are in committee; as of October 22, 2026, none has passed.

Where Wisconsin cannabis law stands (updated October 2026)

As of October 22, 2026, Wisconsin has no adult-use or medical cannabis program. THC outside of hemp is a controlled substance under state law, and the state's own hemp definition still turns on delta-9 THC. People often search for where to buy THC in Wisconsin; there is no state-licensed cannabis store there. Hemp-derived CBD doesn't require a doctor's certification.

Wisconsin's 2025-2026 regular session ended its last general floor period in March 2026. The budget bill, SB 45, became law as 2025 Wisconsin Act 15 without adult-use cannabis. Every other bill below failed to pass on March 23, 2026, under the session schedule (Senate Joint Resolution 1), and none is still alive. The next Legislature is scheduled to start on January 4, 2027. Our read: any of these ideas would need a new bill then.

Bills that would have expanded access

  • AB 50 / SB 45: Governor's 2025-27 budget. Gov. Tony Evers proposed legalizing and taxing adult-use cannabis in his budget. Status: On May 8, 2025, the Joint Committee on Finance took up the budget and adopted its own substitute version; news reports say the cannabis plan was among the items removed. SB 45, as amended, became 2025 Wisconsin Act 15 (signed with partial vetoes July 3, 2025) without adult-use cannabis. AB 50 failed on March 23, 2026. Authors: Joint Committee on Finance, at the request of Gov. Evers.
  • AB 547 / SB 534: Medical cannabis program. Would have created a state medical cannabis program for registered patients, run through licensed dispensaries, with smokable forms excluded. Status: The Senate Health Committee recommended SB 534 on February 5, 2026 (4 to 1). Neither bill got a floor vote, and both failed on March 23, 2026. Authors: Sens. Patrick Testin and Mary Felzkowski; Reps. Pat Snyder, Dean Kaufert, Dan Knodl, Todd Novak, and Paul Melotik.
  • AB 1061 / SB 1045: Adult-use and medical legalization. Would have legalized possession by adults, set up licensed growing, testing, and sales, and cleared or reduced past marijuana convictions. Status: Introduced in late February 2026, referred to committee, and failed on March 23, 2026. Authors: Sen. LaTonya Johnson and 13 other senators; Reps. Darrin Madison, Andrew Hysell, Christian Phelps, and others.

Bills that would have restricted or regulated hemp THC

  • AB 503 / SB 499: Narrower definition of hemp. Would have changed Wisconsin's hemp definition to count total THC and left out lab-altered cannabinoids like delta-8, which would have taken many hemp THC products off Wisconsin shelves. Status: The Assembly State Affairs Committee held a hearing on November 4, 2025. Both bills failed on March 23, 2026. Authors: Sen. Chris Kapenga and Rep. Lindee Rae Brill, with others.
  • AB 747 / SB 682: Rules for hemp THC products. Would have regulated hemp-derived cannabinoid products, including limiting sales to adults 21 and older. These were regulation bills, not a ban. Status: The Senate Agriculture and Revenue Committee sent SB 682 on without a recommendation on February 3, 2026 (4 to 4). Both bills failed on March 23, 2026. Authors: Sen. Patrick Testin and Reps. Rob Kurtz, Shannon Sortwell, Andrew Hysell, and others.
  • AB 680 / SB 644: Age 21 limit for intoxicating hemp. Would have barred people under 21 from buying intoxicating hemp products, with penalties for sellers. Status: Referred to committee and failed on March 23, 2026. Authors: Rep. Christine Sinicki and Sen. LaTonya Johnson, with others.
